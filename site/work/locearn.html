Project 01 · LocEarn

Making outdoor advertising verifiable

Independent, on-ground proof for outdoor campaigns, captured by a distributed rider network. I was the solo designer behind the rider experience, from core flow to V1, validation, V2 and production handoff.

Overview

What is LocEarn?

LocEarn connects brands running outdoor advertising campaigns with people on the ground who can verify those sites. Riders visit assigned locations, capture real-time proof, and get paid for verified submissions.

Problem

Brands had no independent view of what was happening on the ground

The same vendors who owned the ad sites also handled monitoring. Inspections were predictable, and brands had no neutral way to verify what was happening. What if verification came from someone outside the campaign?

Options

Who can verify sites without the vendor knowing?

Vendor networks (same trust problem), field agents (too expensive before any revenue), delivery partnerships, or everyday people incentivised with money. The product also had to earn a rider's trust before asking for effort.

Proof system

Same proof engine, different users

We reused the existing photo verification feature. The job wasn't building the proof system; it was making it work for people who had just seen an Instagram ad. Right place, right time, can't be staged.

V1

We built V1 quickly, then put it in front of riders

We went straight into high fidelity to align stakeholders, then tested the prototype with riders from our existing network. The journey: Discover, Evaluate, Navigate, Verify, Submit. Design decisions, user flow and constraints below.

Feedback

Four friction points in the rider journey

Riders told us the countdown didn't say when to arrive, the next action was unclear, proof and reporting felt disconnected, and onboarding asked too much.

“ltna personal information kyun chahiye?”Why do you need so much personal information?
“Photo lene ke liye exactly kahaan tap karna hai?”Where exactly do I tap to take the photo?
V2

From showing more information to guiding one action at a time

Let riders explore before committing, make tasks easy to compare, make the deadline clear, and make capture the primary action.

Impact

Beta to real-world deployment

The product launched in beta across Delhi NCR before expanding the rider model to West Bengal. Expansion into West Bengal was affected by the state election, which disrupted the planned rollout window.

Reflection

What I'd do differently

  • Design location edge cases earlier — what happens when GPS is inaccurate or the rider isn't detected at the site.
  • Take testing into the rider's context — test while riders are travelling, not just in the office.
  • Define success metrics before iterating — time to first task, task completion and onboarding completion.
