Making outdoor advertising verifiable

01

What is LocEarn?

+

LocEarn connects brands running outdoor advertising campaigns with people on the ground who can verify those sites. Riders visit assigned locations, capture real-time proof, and get paid for verified submissions, giving brands visibility into what's actually happening.

My role

Solo designer. I shaped the rider experience: core flow and product decisions, V1, validating it with riders, iterating, and working with the tech team through production.

The goal

Make campaign verification more reliable and scalable. Brands get trustworthy proof from the ground and can spot issues while the campaign is still running, not after the money is spent.

So we askedWhat if verification came from someone outside the campaign?
Key design challenge

How might we help brands get reliable, on-ground proof at scale? The challenge was a simple enough experience for riders to consistently find, complete and submit verification tasks.

As a brand

“I want independent, real-time proof that my campaigns are live, so I can take action if there are any issues.”

As a rider

“I want to find nearby tasks, know exactly what to do, complete them quickly and get paid without any hassle.”

06

We built V1 quickly, then put it in front of riders

+

We went straight into high fidelity to align stakeholders, then tested the prototype with riders from our existing network, in the office. Their feedback exposed gaps we couldn't see from the screens alone.

Discover→Evaluate→Navigate→Verify→Submit
Key design decisions: three things riders need to know

How do I get there?

Right action at the right time, with a clear arrival time and easy access to maps. Faster decisions, less uncertainty.

What do I need to do?

A simple step-by-step flow to capture proof and complete the task. Easier to complete, especially on the move.

Is it worth it?

Reward and distance upfront, so riders can decide quickly if a task is worth their time and travel.

User flow
The constraints: a few non-negotiables

Proof had to be trustworthy

Location, timestamp and live capture were already part of the system.

Riders are often on the move

They could be travelling to a site and checking the app in between.

Every task has a deadline

Riders needed to reach the site within the allotted time.

Trust starts before the first task

New riders spend their time and fuel before they know if it's worth it.

Four friction points in the rider journey

+
Onboarding

“ltna personal information kyun chahiye?”

Why do you need so much personal information?

“Preferred cities aur permissions abhi kyun select karna hai? Pehle kaam dekhne do.”

Why do I have to select preferred cities and give permissions now? Let me see the tasks first.

“OTP aane mein bahut time lag raha hai.”

The OTP is taking too long to arrive.
Homepage

“Pehle direction loon ya task book karoon?”

Should I check the direction first or book the task?

“Mujhe bas paas ke tasks dekhne hain, range samajhna thoda confusing hai.”

I just want nearby tasks; the range is a little confusing.

“Iss page pe direction kyu dikha rahe? Aur dono buttons ka color bhi same hai.”

Why are directions shown on this page? Both buttons are the same colour too.
Task details

“Abhi mujhe kya karna hai? Direction loon ya task start karoon?”

I'm not sure what I'm supposed to do now. Should I get directions or start the task?

“35 minute bache hain, par mujhe kitne baje tak site pe pahunchna hai?”

It says 35 minutes remaining, but by what time do I need to reach the site?
Submit proof

“Photo lene ke liye exactly kahaan tap karna hai?”

Where exactly do I tap to take the photo?

“Mujhe bas site ki photo leni hai, itna sab kyun dikh raha hai?”

I just need to take the site photo, why is there so much on the screen?

“Report site bhi tha kya, mujhe toh dikha hi nahi.”

I totally missed that there is a 'Report Site' option.

08

From more information to one action at a time

+
The redesign · 01

Let riders see the value before asking for commitment

1
Let riders explore firstUnderstand the app and available tasks before completing a full profile.
2
Complete details when neededNon-essential information moves later in the journey, when it becomes relevant.
3
Move profile completion laterAdditional information is collected when relevant, not as a block to task discovery.
Trade-offFaster time-to-value vs. complete information upfront. We collect less upfront and ask only when it becomes necessary.
The redesign · 02

Make the decision easier before the rider starts travelling

1
Make tasks easy to compareRiders quickly compare what each task pays before deciding where to go.
2
Reduce the effort to find a taskNearby tasks are easier to scan within a practical travel distance.
3
One visual hierarchyReward, distance and action sit together.
Trade-offMore tasks visible vs. less detail per task. We reduced card-level detail to help riders scan more opportunities within range.
The redesign · 03

The action changed with the rider's state

1
Make the deadline clear“Arrive by” gives riders a clear target while they're on the move.
2
Design for the journeyNavigation, time remaining and the site image stay visible while riders check on the move.
3
Make actions state-based“Open Maps” is primary while travelling. “Start Task” unlocks only on reaching the site.
Trade-offMore guidance during the journey vs. a simpler task screen. We added context and state-based actions without making the rider work out what's next.
The redesign · 04

Make the proof submission flow easier to follow

1
Make capture the primary actionIt's immediately clear where to start capturing proof.
2
Show what has been capturedRiders see submitted photos and the remaining slots before moving on.
3
Site reporting is part of the flowCapture proof and report site issues in the same task flow.
Trade-offGuided flow vs. flexibility. We prioritised a focused sequence for the most common path, giving less prominence to secondary capture options.

11

What I'd do differently

+

V2 solved the problems we could see. These are the gaps I'd address next.

01

Design location edge cases earlier

V2 made “Start Task” depend on reaching the site. Next, define what happens when GPS is inaccurate, unavailable, or the rider is there but not detected.

02

Take testing into the rider's context

Office-based reviews found major issues. Next, test while riders are actually travelling, navigating and completing a task.

03

Define success metrics before iterating

We drove V2 from feedback but didn't instrument the V1 to V2 change. Next time: time to first task, task completion and onboarding completion, before rollout.
