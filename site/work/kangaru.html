Project 03 · KangaruCase study · 2026

A smarter way to power last-mile delivery

Kangaru is an EV-powered last-mile delivery platform that owns and operates its fleet. I designed the rider experience from onboarding to the first unlock, and redesigned it mid-project when investment changed the business.

RoleProduct designer
Duration2 months, brief to final
TeamFounders, 1 developer, 1 designer (me)
StatusReal product · In development
01Opportunity

Everyone built the vehicle. We built what runs on top.

EV adoption in last-mile delivery was growing fast, but most of the attention was on vehicles, batteries and charging infrastructure. We saw an opportunity to build the software that could make an EV rider's day easier: fleet companies would buy it and their riders would use it.

01

Earnings

No single view of what riders were earning across delivery platforms.

02

Charging

No EV-specific guidance for finding chargers, planning range or understanding charging time.

03

Vehicle health

Limited visibility into battery and vehicle health.

Our thinking behind V1
01

Market gap

B2B model

V1 thesisOwn the software layer of a hardware-first industry.
02Realisation

The product had a ceiling

We were only seeing how far our assumptions could take us.

01

It wouldn't scale

Software sold to fleet companies was limited by the size of the fleet market.

02

One decision from irrelevance

A fleet could eventually build similar software in-house, leaving us with little ownership of the experience.

03

Distribution was the bottleneck

We depended on fleet companies to bring riders onto the product, making growth hostage to a long B2B sales cycle.

03Pivot

V1 got us to the door. Investment opened it.

We always knew software alone wasn't enough. The direction changed when investment came in and we would now own the fleet directly.

We kept building and started looking for investment

Not because V1 was failing, but because we knew what was possible with the right backing.

Investment validated our belief

It confirmed the market for EV fleets was bigger, faster growing, and had far fewer players.

A bigger opportunity became clear

Not selling software to vehicle owners, but owning the vehicles ourselves and building the software on top.

We shifted our direction

With the backing in place, we moved to building and owning our own EV fleet.

What changed, and what didn't

The direction shifted, but the design problems stayed the same.

Ownership

V1Fleet companies like Yulu and Baaz owned the vehicles. We sold them software.

V2Kangaru owned the fleet directly.

Relationship

V1The rider only used our software.

V2The rider opened the Kangaru app and knew exactly who they were working with.

Revenue

V1We charged fleet companies a monthly subscription.

V2We earned 3% per delivery directly from the rider's earnings.

Brand

V1Kangaru was invisible.

V2Kangaru became the face of the experience.

05Key screens

Designing for riders who need to feel safe enough to act

Onboarding & KYC

Verified was more important than fast

Before accessing a vehicle, we had to know who they were.

1
Progress bar on KYCTriggers the goal-gradient effect: the closer they feel to finishing, the more likely they complete it.
2
Instructions before captureRiders know what a good photo looks like before they take it, which reduces bad submissions.
3
Camera or uploadKYC just needs a valid ID. Forcing live capture for a static document adds friction with no benefit.
Homepage

Nearest hub, auto-selected

Finding a hub shouldn't require thinking.

1
Map over listHumans think spatially. The faster a rider finds a hub, the faster they start earning.
2
The hub card shows what mattersDistance, time and vehicle count are visible before the rider taps anything.
3
One clear action per hubEach card has one CTA. The rider picks the closest hub and moves forward.
Vehicle selection

Pick up the vehicle, in one tap

Riders choose which vehicle to go with.

1
Available / Charging badgeRiders see instantly which vehicles can be booked. No guessing.
2
Ghost button on charging vehiclesA disabled state, so riders understand it's unavailable without us hiding it.
3
A CTA on every cardNo select-then-confirm. See a vehicle, book it directly. One less tap.
Security deposit

The business model, made visible

Riders have been burned by hidden fees before. This screen had one job: remove that fear before they pay.

1
“Pay once, top up only when needed”The first thing they see, before the amount.
2
Payment breakdown over a single totalRiders understand exactly what they're paying for. No surprises.
3
Refund message before the amountRiders know the remaining balance comes back when they exit.
Booking confirmation

Payment done. Now what?

Most confirmation screens stop at “payment successful.” We didn't.

1
Transaction ID upfrontProof of payment the moment it goes through.
2
All details in one placeVehicle, pickup time, location and amount, so riders don't have to go back and check.
3
“Ready to unlock” hintTells the rider exactly what to do next, instead of closing the app and wondering where to go.

07Learnings

Design doesn't start with screens

01

Business model is a design problem

The deposit, the 3% fee, wallet transparency. I couldn't separate the two, and shouldn't have to.

02

Ambiguity is the job

V1 became V2 mid-project. No brief. No playbook. That's the work.

03

The rider is always the starting point

Every screen came back to one question: does this make the rider feel safe enough to act?
