01Context

We already had the data. We saw a product in it.

01

The opportunity

Brands and agencies plan OOH campaigns in new cities. Before they start, they want to know which competitors are already advertising there, but that information is scattered across vendors and takes manual effort to find.

02

The data we already had

LOCAD had a rich database of OOH sites. Site details and photos, the brands at each site, categories, locations with coordinates, and capture dates.

03

What we thought of building

A map and analytics experience that lets planners view competitor presence across locations, categories and time, using LOCAD's existing data.

SitesBrandsCategoriesLocationsDates
The questionWhat if brands could use this data to understand where competitors are already active?
02Opportunity

From data to product opportunity

The opportunity went beyond OOH sites. The goal was to make the relevant competitive context easier to understand for brands and agencies.

01

Who

Which brands are already active in the market? See which advertisers are present across locations and categories.

02

Where

Where are those brands concentrated across the city? Understand the geographic presence of competitors.

03

How much

How strong is their presence? Compare the number and distribution of sites to understand competitive intensity.

04

When

How has their presence changed over time? Compare activity across time periods to see trends.

Product boundaryCompetition Tracking gives brands and agencies competitive context. It doesn't recommend where they should run their next campaign.
03Directions

Four directions, one decision

With the underlying data and client feedback in mind, I mapped possible product directions and discussed them with clients to see which matched their needs best.

01

Market Exploration Tool

Understand what is happening across a city or market. Too broad for initial needs; clients wanted competitor-specific insights.

Not at this stage
02

Brand Footprint

Understand where a particular brand is present. Useful, but clients wanted to compare against competitors, not just view one brand.

Not at this stage
03

Site Intelligence

Understand the brands and activity around sites. Valuable at site level, but didn't address the need for a broader competitive view.

Not at this stage
04

Competition Tracking

Understand where competing brands within a category are active. Aligned with client requirements and made the best use of the data LOCAD already had.

Selected direction

05Testing

Filters were in the way, and two views weren't enough

User testing with Dalmia Cements surfaced two problems with the first version.

Finding 01 · The filter panel's position

Users found it hard to explore the map because the filter panel took too much space, reducing the area available for the map.

“I have to keep going back to the left to change filters. Since my main focus is the map, I'd rather have more space to explore it.”

Disrupted exploration flow: switching focus from the map to the left panel interrupted analysis. Filters felt secondary.

“I'd prefer filters at the top or a collapsible panel so I can stay focused on the map.”

Dalmia Cements
Finding 02 · Only two analysis options

The initial version had just two analysis options, which limited how users could explore the data.

“I can see the brands and cities, but I think I'm still missing the bigger picture. I'd like to know how growth is changing week by week and which states are driving it.”

No growth trend view, and no state-level context.

“Can I download the data I'm looking at?”

Users wanted to export what they were analysing.

07Analytics

We turned raw activity into competitive signals

Once users found competitor activity on the map, the next challenge was helping them understand what the data meant: who was active, and how it was changing over time.

01 · Track change

Growth helps users identify shifts in competitive presence over time. We paired a scannable ranking with a trend visualisation, so users see which brands are gaining or losing presence and understand the change over time.

02 · Compare competitors

Brand-level analysis lets users understand competitive presence at a glance. A ranked table makes brands easy to compare, while the breakdown chart shows how their presence is distributed across media types.

08Filters

Making the right data discoverable

The idea was simple: let users narrow a large OOH dataset down to the competition they actually want to understand.

01 · Set the market

Choose the city and location you want to investigate.

02 · Narrow the search

Filter competitor activity by brand, category, media type and date.

03 · Spot the patterns

See where competitors are active and identify clusters worth exploring.
